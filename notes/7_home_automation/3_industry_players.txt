3  --  Who builds this in the market

Four broad groups build home-automation controllers, aimed at different customers.


1)  DIY (Do It Yourself) open-source

  -  Home Assistant (Python, community-driven, 3000+ brand integrations).

  -  openHAB (Java, similar goal).

Made for HOBBYISTS (people who do this for fun in their free time) and PROSUMERS (advanced home users  --  the word is a mix of "professional" and "consumer", meaning more skilled than an average user but not a paid professional). These users install the software on their own machine and keep it running themselves.

Good side: it is free, easy to change the way you want, and has a very large user community online to ask for help.

Bad side: no company gives official paid support if something breaks. Also, the small driver programs that connect to each brand's devices can suddenly stop working with no warning  --  for example, when the brand pushes an update on its side and the community driver has not caught up yet.


2)  Big-tech consumer

  -  Google Home
  -  Apple HomeKit
  -  Amazon Alexa

Aimed at the average consumer. Very simple to set up, cloud-heavy, limited brand support, no professional installation required.


3)  Premium commercial integrators

  -  Crestron
  -  Control4
  -  Savant
  -  RTI

Aimed at high-end residential and commercial buildings, sold only through authorised dealers. Closed ecosystems, premium price, deep customisation, strong professional support.


4)  Professional integrator segment

This is where companies like Vyom sit. The customer is a mid-to-high-end home that wants:

  -  one app for the whole home,
  -  reliable local control,
  -  KNX-first wiring (the standard used by most Indian and European premium installations),
  -  a technician-friendly dashboard for commissioning,
  -  full control over hardware, software and updates.

Vyom's own box  --  called "okas_box"  --  started by using Home Assistant under the hood. The team is now writing its own Python engine so it can control the full supply chain, add KNX Secure natively, work fully offline on the local network, sign every configuration bundle from cloud to box, isolate each driver so a buggy one cannot crash the whole box, and enforce hardening rules learned from real production issues.
